Design principles

01

The whole network, always

Every road in OpenStreetMap stays on the map. RoadMind condition data is a colour layer on top, never a filter that hides roads.

02

Honest about what it doesn't know

A road without data is grey “No Data”. It is never scored good or bad, and routes are neither rewarded nor penalised for it.

03

Calm, premium, accessible

Soft lavender surfaces, deep-navy type, large rounded cards, AA-contrast text and colour that is always paired with a label.

04

Clarity before density

One primary action per screen, plain-language results and AI estimates that say they are estimates.

Citizen experience · web

Home, report, AI result, interactive map, route planner and route recommendation — the journey from “I saw a pothole” to “I took a safer road”.

Who are you? — two doors

The first screen asks the visitor to choose. Users and Admin & Road Maintenance staff enter through visibly different pages, never one combined form. The staff door is a single sign-in page for administrators and maintenance employees: the server checks the password, account status, verified email and role, and the role it finds picks the dashboard. Its front page is the Admin Portal: Login for existing accounts, Create Account to request one. A request grants nothing — the email is verified first, then an existing administrator approves or rejects it.

User door

Bright and friendly: road imagery, Login, Create User Account or Continue as Guest.

Admin & Road Maintenance door

Dark and security-focused. Login for existing accounts, a request form for new ones.

After approval & sign-in

Administrators decide who gets in; maintenance staff see only the roads assigned to them.

Administrator workspace

Sidebar navigation, eight headline numbers, three charts and a Top 5 maintenance table; roads are assigned to maintenance staff from the Maintenance page.

Mobile

The same system at 390 px: bottom navigation (Home, Map, Report, Routes, Profile) with a raised Report button, full-bleed map with a bottom sheet, single-column cards. The role selection, the Admin Portal and both sign-in doors collapse to one column too.

What these screens are, and are not

  • Screenshots of the real running app. Roads, place names and map tiles are real OpenStreetMap data (Chennai sample area).
  • The condition colours, reports, severity and risk numbers are simulated demo data on roughly 18% of the roads — the rest are grey “No Data”, exactly as the product intends.
  • No mail server is configured by default, so verification, invitation, approval and reset “emails” are written to backend/data/outbox/. The portal screenshots come from a scratch install with demo accounts (“Demo Administrator”, gmail.com / example.org addresses made up for the shots) - nothing in the product is pre-created, and the requests shown in Users were made through the Create Account form.
  • Damage detection here is a heuristic OpenCV detector and the risk model is trained on synthetic data; both say so in the UI. Statistics on the home page are live numbers, not marketing placeholders. The hero is an illustration (drop frontend/public/hero.jpg to use a photo).
